Experiment 10. Determination of State of Charge 
(SoC) 
Importance of SoC: 
1. As we know, unlike the Li-ion batteries in other countries, in India, the batteries 
often experience issues such as explosion due to poor thermal management and 
battery assembly during usage. Hence, it is very important to understand the 
battery chemistry before assembling the bulk battery pack. 
2. The existing battery management and assembly system is not suitable for all 
conditions due to the poor understanding of battery chemistry. 
3. The state of charge is the vital one for all kinds of battery manufacturing. Hence, 
it is important to understand battery chemistry because the state of charge is 
really important. 
4. Currently, the successful battery cathode material or battery cathodes available 
in the market are Lithium iron phosphate and Lithium Nickel Manganese Oxide-
based cathodes. However, there are still lots of other types of battery chemistry 
(cathode materials available). Hence, it is very important to understand the 
battery chemistry of Li-ion batteries. 
Aim: 
The evaluation of the state of charge (SoC) for the Li-ion battery using different 
types of battery cathode materials (various battery chemistries) through the open 
circuit voltage (OCV), current, and resistance with respect to the discharging and 
rest time. 
Principle: 
State-of-charge is generally defined as the available amount of charge in a given 
battery (Q) related to the maximum available amount of charge, which can be taken 
from this battery after a 100% full charging ( C), and is usually expressed as a 
percentage:

Apparatus required: 
1. The equipment and connectors required for this experiment 
2. The batteries of Lithium iron phosphate and lithium manganese oxide 
cathodes-based battery of either 18650 or 23650 model. 
 
3. The holder is to get the positive and negative connections. 
4. The light-emitting diode of a 2.5 to 3.7-volt range of operations 
 
 
 
 
 
 
5. A voltmeter and an ammeter 
 
6. A button switch 
 
Procedure: 
Assemble the single cell battery of two different chemistries, say A and B, with the 
respective holders, switches, and LED load. To measure the open circuit voltage, we 
will connect the batteries in series to the multimeter (see Fig.1) and measure the 
voltage initially and take it over time at o. Then arrange the multimeter in such a way 
to measure the flow of electrons and switch on the biased LED load as shown in the 
figure, and measure the current. After 5 minutes, switch off the circuit and rest for 5 
minutes. Then measure the OCV at time 5 minutes and subsequently proceed to take 
the reading for 30-45 minutes and tabulate the results with OCV, current, and SoC for 
the taken time in Table 1.
